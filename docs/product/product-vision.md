# Product vision

## 1. Product vision

This portfolio is a professional product designed to demonstrate my capabilities through evidence, context, and technical reasoning. It presents me as a Frontend Engineer specialised in React and TypeScript, with engineering experience across the wider software stack when required. It explains the reasoning behind selected decisions and shows how I build maintainable, accessible, and well-tested products.

The portfolio itself is treated as a production software project. Its documentation, architecture, testing strategy, and development workflow are intentionally visible as evidence of engineering practice.

It should offer more professional depth than a traditional CV or generic portfolio template. It is a living representation of my professional development and should evolve alongside my experience, projects, and engineering judgment.

> Build a professional portfolio that shows not only what I have worked on, but how I approach frontend engineering, product development, and technical decision-making.

## 2. Product purpose

The portfolio exists to:

- Help recruiters and hiring managers quickly understand my professional value.
- Give technical evaluators evidence of my engineering judgment.
- Present selected professional experience without exposing confidential information.
- Document personal projects through their context, decisions, trade-offs, and results.
- Demonstrate end-to-end ownership of a production-ready digital product.
- Support future job applications, networking, and professional conversations.

Its purpose is not merely to reproduce my CV. It should connect my experience and skills to concrete evidence and explain how I approach engineering work.

## 3. Target audience

### Primary audience

- Technical recruiters hiring frontend or product engineers.
- Engineering managers and technical leads.
- Frontend engineers participating in technical evaluations.
- Companies looking for strong React and TypeScript expertise.

This audience needs to understand my specialisation, the scope and relevance of my experience, and how I contribute to product and engineering outcomes. It expects concise professional context supported by selected projects, technical decisions, trade-offs, testing practices, accessibility considerations, and examples of ownership and collaboration.

### Secondary audience

- Other software engineers.
- Potential collaborators.
- Professional contacts and people reached through networking.
- People interested in my projects, technical writing, or engineering approach.

Content should remain understandable to non-specialist recruiters while providing enough depth for engineers to assess the reasoning and evidence behind the work.

## 4. Professional positioning

**Frontend Engineer specialised in React and TypeScript, focused on scalable, accessible, maintainable and well-tested digital products.**

Frontend engineering is the main area of my professional experience. I also have two years of full-stack experience using React and TypeScript alongside Node.js, Express, and PostgreSQL. This broader experience helps me understand the systems that frontend applications depend on and collaborate effectively beyond the interface when required.

This positioning reflects that I:

- Understand product and domain complexity, not only UI implementation.
- Value strict typing, clear boundaries, modularity, and extensible component architecture.
- Treat accessibility, automated testing, and maintainability as standard engineering practices.
- Can contribute to technical direction and cross-team improvements.
- Have experience working on production systems with demanding quality requirements.
- Can contribute across the wider software stack when a product or team requires it.

The portfolio should not position me as a visual designer, a generic full-stack expert, an AI engineer, a freelancer offering commercial services, or a specialist in technologies for which it provides no evidence.

## 5. First-impression takeaways

Within the first few seconds, a visitor should understand that:

1. I am an experienced Frontend Engineer.
2. React and TypeScript are central to my professional profile.
3. Component architecture, strict typing, and extensibility are central to how I build software.
4. I work with production-quality standards.
5. Accessibility, testing, and maintainability are part of my normal engineering process.
6. I can explain technical decisions and trade-offs.
7. I understand the wider software stack when the work requires it.
8. The portfolio contains evidence rather than unsupported claims.

## 6. Visitor actions

### Primary actions

- Review selected projects or case studies.
- Understand my professional experience and engineering approach.
- Contact me regarding a relevant professional opportunity.

### Secondary actions

- View or download my CV.
- Visit my GitHub or LinkedIn profile.
- Read technical documentation or engineering notes.
- Explore the source code and development process of the portfolio itself.

These actions define intended outcomes, not specific controls, labels, or page placements.

## 7. Core value proposition

I combine frontend specialisation with engineering discipline, product awareness, and experience working on complex production systems. My work is grounded in strong React and TypeScript foundations, strict typing, maintainable code, and scalable, extensible component architecture.

I treat accessibility as a product quality requirement and automated testing as a source of confidence in changes. I can reason clearly about trade-offs, collaborate across teams, and contribute to shared technical improvements rather than working only within isolated features. This combination of engineering practices, technical quality, and product awareness is the core value the portfolio should demonstrate.

## 8. Differentiators

- Real-world experience in a complex and regulated product domain.
- Ability to work beyond isolated UI components.
- Professional experience contributing across frontend, backend, API, and persistence concerns.
- Experience with dynamic forms and complex user flows.
- Strong React and TypeScript foundations.
- Experience designing scalable and extensible component architectures.
- Accessibility-aware implementation.
- Strongly typed engineering practices.
- Automated testing as part of delivery.
- Experience with shared migrations and cross-team initiatives.
- Documentation of architectural and product decisions.
- Treating the portfolio itself as a professionally managed software product.

These differentiators should be supported with factual examples. They should not rely on inflated seniority, invented results, or unsupported metrics.

## 9. Content required

The minimum content needed to support this vision is:

- A clear professional introduction.
- Concise professional positioning.
- Selected experience presented through responsibilities, challenges, and contributions.
- Project case studies.
- Explanations of technical decisions and trade-offs.
- Skills supported by evidence.
- Contact information.
- Links to GitHub, LinkedIn, and a CV.
- An accessibility statement.
- Documentation about the portfolio's architecture and development process.
- Appropriate legal and privacy information if analytics or data collection are introduced later.

Not all of this content must be implemented in the initial release. Delivery can be incremental as long as each addition supports the vision and maintains an accurate professional representation.

### Repository as engineering evidence

The repository is part of the portfolio, not only the place where its source code is stored. It should make the engineering process visible through:

- Architectural decision records that explain important choices and trade-offs.
- Issues and milestones that show how work is planned and scoped.
- Pull request templates that encourage consistent review and delivery practices.
- Product and technical documentation that preserves context over time.
- Automated tests that provide confidence in changes.
- Continuous integration and quality gates as they are introduced.

These practices should provide evidence of technical quality, ownership, and engineering discipline without prescribing the specific tools or architecture used to implement them.

## 10. Out of scope

- A complete chronological reproduction of the CV.
- Confidential employer or client information.
- Internal names, repositories, screenshots, source code, or business data.
- Unsupported performance metrics or business results.
- Personal lifestyle content unrelated to the professional objective.
- A blog requiring continuous publication for the initial release.
- Testimonials that have not been explicitly authorised.
- Freelance service pricing or commercial sales pages.
- Detailed visual design decisions.
- Framework or infrastructure selection.
- A detailed sitemap or component specification.
- Exhaustive coverage of every technology I have ever used.

## 11. Product principles

- **Evidence over claims.** Support professional statements with relevant examples and context.
- **Clarity over unnecessary complexity.** Make the product's purpose and content easy to understand.
- **Accessibility by default.** Treat inclusive access as a baseline quality requirement.
- **Quality over feature quantity.** Prefer a smaller set of complete, credible content and capabilities.
- **Professional depth without confidential detail.** Explain meaningful work without exposing protected information.
- **Progressive disclosure for mixed audiences.** Provide an accessible overview with optional technical depth.
- **Maintainable content and implementation.** Keep the portfolio accurate and sustainable as it evolves.
- **Honest representation of experience.** Avoid inflated positioning, unsupported claims, and misleading omissions.

## 12. Success indicators

Initial success is indicated when:

- A recruiter can quickly identify my role and specialisation.
- A technical visitor can find evidence of engineering judgment.
- Visitors can understand the relevance of each selected project.
- Visitors can easily reach professional profiles, the CV, and contact options.
- Portfolio content remains accurate and maintainable.
- No confidential professional information is exposed.

These indicators are intentionally qualitative. Measurable analytics targets may be defined later if appropriate data collection is introduced.

## 13. Decision boundaries

This document defines product direction, but it does not decide:

- Visual identity.
- Layout.
- Information architecture in detail.
- Technology stack.
- Hosting.
- Analytics provider.
- Component architecture.
- Final copywriting.

Those decisions belong to future issues and should remain aligned with this product vision.
